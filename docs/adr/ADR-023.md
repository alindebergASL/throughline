## Decision

### PO-1 — Predicate registry and cardinality

B2 uses product-owned registry version `truth-predicate-catalog.v1`. Its complete initial set is
exactly `activity.outcome` for Activity subjects and `initiative.primary_objective` for Initiative
subjects. Both accept only non-empty canonical NFC text and are single-valued. Every registry entry
declares its allowed subject kind, value schema/canonicalizer, equality and conflict comparators,
cardinality, owner authority scope, and deterministic-view treatment. Unknown predicates, wrong
subject kinds, and noncanonical values fail closed. At most one AcceptedFact may occupy the active
`current` or `contested` coordinate for a tenant/workspace/subject/predicate; Claims and terminal
history remain unlimited. The catalog version is part of command identity and deterministic-view
revision inputs. These predicates do not duplicate Activity/Initiative stage, status, health, or
canonical Relationship edges, and single cardinality is only the bounded B2 policy.

### PO-2 — Contest and resolution semantics

Conflict detection alone never changes accepted truth. Explicit `fact.contest` links one
mechanically supported incompatible Claim to the active AcceptedFact, changes that same Fact to
`contested`, changes/links the challenger Claim as `conflicted`, and creates immutable conflict and
lifecycle evidence. It never creates a second AcceptedFact.

Resolution is explicit and exhaustive: `fact.uphold` rejects the challenger with a recorded reason
and rationale and restores the existing Fact to `current`; `fact.supersede` atomically identifies a
new replacement Fact and leaves the former Fact `superseded`; `fact.revoke` leaves the former Fact
`revoked` and no active value. Accept and supersede do not conceal contest, uphold, or revoke.
Every transition preserves actor User, active Membership, authority basis, policy, reason,
rationale, conflict linkage, and history. An audience sees contested state only when authorized to
see the contest; an inaccessible challenger cannot affect visible titles, counts, citations,
revision identities, or presentation.

### PO-3 — Lifecycle authority and emergency action

Proposal requires a complete human with an active Membership, live contribution authority, and
access to both subject and evidence. Routine lifecycle authority is subject-specific: the Activity
owner for Activity facts and Initiative owner for Initiative facts. A graph Person grants no
authority unless it resolves to the actor's active Membership in the current Workspace. Membership,
policy version, ownership, subject access, and evidence access are reauthorized in the eventual
command transaction. An ordinary Workspace admin is not automatically a truth approver, and B2
adds no generic approver delegation.

A human Workspace owner has only two distinct emergency command shapes:
`fact.emergency_contest` and `fact.emergency_revoke`. There is no emergency accept, uphold, or
supersede. Emergency action requires an enumerated legal/compliance, safety, security-incident, or
material-truth-risk reason and a non-empty bounded rationale; it does not bypass RLS, evidence
verification, access classification, optimistic locking, idempotency, audit, or outbox duties.
Nonexistent and unauthorized targets remain outwardly indistinguishable.

### PO-4 — Source correction and revalidation

A correction appends a successor SourceArtifact and never rewrites its predecessor. Existing spans,
Claims, Facts, and citations are never copied, remapped, or rebound to the successor. Correction
invalidates affected deterministic views. If corrected evidence was a Fact's sole active support,
the Fact becomes `contested` with machine-readable reason `source_corrected` and is presented to an
authorized audience as requiring review, not as clean current truth. A Fact may remain current only
when independent active evidence supports the identical canonical value and sustains its recorded
confidence; obsolete citations are excluded and access is never lowered in place.

Revalidation requires a new mechanically verified Claim from the successor and ordinary human
authority. Same-value replacement is immutable supersession/revalidation history and may later be
presented as reverified. Correcting the source alone never establishes replacement truth.

### PO-5 — Retention, tombstone, and erasure

B2 follows the governing B1 retention policy and actual hash-disposition decision. Tombstone or
lawful erasure removes or cryptographically erases all covered content-bearing B2 copies, including
source excerpts, Claim text/values, covered Fact payloads, citations, deterministic-view content,
and any in-scope cache or queued payload. Only the minimum policy-permitted tombstone/audit envelope
remains. Raw hashes are not retained by default when policy requires erasure; any permitted retained
hash remains sensitive and purpose-limited.

Sole-support erasure revokes the Fact and redacts its payload where required. An independently
supported Fact remains only when surviving evidence and policy both permit it. Erasure never lowers
access in place, and ordinary readers cannot infer erased content through identifiers, errors,
history, counts, citations, or conflict state. This PR defines reason/evidence contracts only; it
does not add storage systems or lifecycle handlers.

### PO-6 — AcceptedFact confidence

All selected support for one acceptance must agree on canonical subject, predicate, value, text,
and validity. AcceptedFact confidence is fixed and audited at acceptance. The default is the
strongest selected valid Claim using `confirmed > strong > weak > unknown`; Claim count never boosts
confidence. A human acceptor may choose a strictly lower confidence only with a recorded enumerated
reason and rationale and may never raise it above the strongest support.

Later support changes never silently recompute stored confidence. A material confidence change uses
supersession. If surviving evidence can no longer sustain the recorded confidence, the Fact requires
review while retaining its recorded value. Confidence remains supporting detail; ratification,
evidence, recency, and review need lead ordinary presentation.

## PR 1 contract consequence

The explicit domain vocabulary is `claim.propose`, `fact.accept`, `fact.contest`, `fact.uphold`,
`fact.supersede`, `fact.revoke`, `fact.emergency_contest`, `fact.emergency_revoke`, and
`derived_view.regenerate`. PR 1 supplies strict parsers, canonical identities, immutable types,
verified-evidence admission, and pure confidence/reason rules only. It adds no execution,
authorization, persistence, audit/outbox writer, source-correction integration, B3 runtime, model,
provider, renderer, route, or UI.

## Consequences

- Later persistence and command execution must consume these exact contracts and repeat live
  authorization inside their transactions.
- A VerifiedClaimSourceSpan and an AcceptedFact have construction-controlled branded boundaries;
  callers cannot structurally manufacture either through the package root.
- Any change to the predicate catalog, command identity, authority matrix, lifecycle vocabulary,
  source reconciliation, retention semantics, or confidence rule requires a superseding ADR and new
  golden fixtures.
